# Broad Historical Timeline

**Status: Approved setting baseline**

## Dating Convention

Dates in this section use **BCD** (*Before Current Day*). `0 BCD` is the story's present. The convention is an author-facing convenience, not yet an in-universe calendar. Dates are deliberately broad; their relative order and duration matter more than an exact count.

## Historical Reading of the Present

This is neither a newly opened frontier nor an ancient, fully settled galactic order. FTL is 260 years old, but routine short travel on mature corridors has existed for only about a century. The model supports a civilisation in which a major vessel's crew treats interstellar travel, mixed-species employment, and long-duration missions as ordinary enough to plan around, but treats a multi-generational stranded return as a profound social and logistical rupture.

Reconnection must not be read as a universal erasure of distance. The selected numerical model gives a characteristic maintained-route extent of about 820 ly, a durable settlement extent of about 790 ly, and selected exploration corridors reaching about 1,230 ly. These are irregular, core-centred clusters and routes, not filled spheres or hard borders. The remaining FTL limits in [[99 Workshop/02 Spacetime Engineering|Spacetime Engineering]] still determine communications, navigation risk, mass movement, and political reach.

## Selected Spatial Scale

| Region | Approximate radius from the historical core | Character |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| True core | 0--50 ly | Earth, neighbouring systems, major old trade nodes, and dense institutional and industrial capacity. |
| Integrated old core | 50--175 ly | Mature routes, capital, shipbuilding, research, and high political weight. |
| Mature sublight diaspora | 175--390 ly | Old, varied, locally entrenched societies whose histories predate FTL. |
| Reconnection settlement belt | 390--800 ly | The principal FTL-era expansion and integration zone: connected but unequal and politically uneven. |
| Sparse settlement frontier | 800--1,200 ly | Isolated recent communities, specialised projects, extraction sites, and contact settlements. |
| Exploration and contact corridors | To about 1,230 ly | Selectively surveyed spokes rather than continuous, routinely inhabited space. |

## Consequences Already Implied by This Shape

- **Near-universal only within connected space:** shared technical standards and political vocabulary can become widespread without representing all settled societies equally.
- **Broadly typical:** many present institutions, families, professions, and markets have some reconnection-era history; their claims to universality remain contestable.
- **Locally variable:** isolation duration, technical inheritance, biological adaptation, wealth, and contact experience differ enough that no single human or non-human history can stand for the whole Milky Way.
- Human-led power plausibly rests on accumulated population, capital, and infrastructure from the older diaspora, not on a claim that other people are less capable of self-government.
- The baseline does not determine the age of any particular polity, the number of inhabited systems, or the speed and capacity of FTL communications. Those remain dependencies for later questions in [[99 Workshop/05 Milky Way Astropolitical Foundations|Milky Way Astropolitical Foundations]].

## Decisions Still Required

- Is the FTL breakthrough concentrated in one polity, jointly developed, or assembled from several discoveries?
- Which actors control or maintain the route infrastructure that enables the speed difference between the true core and the frontier?
- How do the selected travel and settlement scales appear in the crew's education, family histories, rituals, and political assumptions?
