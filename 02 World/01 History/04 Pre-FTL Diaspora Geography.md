# Pre-FTL Diaspora Geography

**Status: Approved setting baseline**

## Scope

This document owns the selected answer to point 2 of [[99 Workshop/05 Milky Way Astropolitical Foundations|Milky Way Astropolitical Foundations]]: the scale, historical layering, and character of the human diaspora before FTL reconnection. It describes a civilisational geography, not a complete star map, polity list, or population census.

## Selected Model

Before FTL, human-descended and post-human settlement reaches a characteristic maximum of about **390 ly from Sol**. This is neither a filled sphere nor a uniform frontier. It is an uneven network of dense local clusters, partially connected settlement chains, wide gaps, isolated systems, and a few exceptional long-distance foundations.

The model distinguishes three different speeds:

| Process | Working value | Meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Information exchange | `1c` | Signals, authenticated archives, cultural material, research, and correspondence travel at light speed. |
| Ordinary durable settlement growth | about `0.05c` | The historical rate at which sustained expansion can advance after accounting for industrial preparation, migration, founding, failure, and support. |
| Rare first-order foundation transit | up to about `0.1c` | Exceptional Sol-backed expeditions to deliberately selected systems; these are not routine traffic or a general expansion rate. |

The `0.05c` figure is a macroscopic civilisational average, not a promise that every colony launches another at that pace forever. The binding limits are willing migrants, capital, local industrial maturity, technical expertise, risk tolerance, and the perceived value of another multi-generational project. Successful centres usually deepen their own systems and nearby networks before, instead of automatically, extending an endless outward wave.

## Settlement Genealogy

Sol and its immediate neighbours form the oldest, most mutually legible region. They are politically and culturally distinct, but share unusually deep historical ties, technical standards, archives, family connections, and institutional reference points.

Over the early millennia, Sol sponsors a limited number of high-priority foundation expeditions: plausibly a dozen to twenty projects with unusual capital, archival support, and effective transit speed. Some fail, remain small, or lose their original relation to Sol. Roughly **four to seven** become major legacy civilisation-complexes: regional networks with one or more centres and their own associated worlds, habitats, technical traditions, breakaways, and political histories. They are not presumed to be unitary states.

Those centres seed smaller local bubbles. Later waves are more locally financed, less coordinated, and less likely to retain meaningful practical ties to the historical core. The result is recursive rather than radial: a core of old mutual familiarity; several historically important first-order centres; smaller second- and third-order clusters; and, at the far fringe, systems that are effectively on their own.

## Characteristic Layers

| Layer | Approximate distance from Sol | Pre-FTL character |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Solar neighbourhood | 0--15 ly | The most integrated historical region. Its systems remain plural, but shared public life, archives, technical language, and slow correspondence are unusually durable. |
| Foundational inner diaspora | 15--80 ly | Early direct colonies and close settlement clusters, most with a recognisable Sol-derived inheritance despite thousands of years of local change. |
| Legacy civilisation belt | 80--220 ly | The principal homes of the four to seven major civilisation-complexes. Their regional influence and independent institutions predate FTL by millennia. |
| Secondary diaspora | 220--320 ly | Smaller regional clusters, prosperous standalone systems, partial settlement chains, and long-lived local societies whose connection to Sol is chiefly historical or archival. |
| Outer sublight fringe | 320--390 ly | Sparse, uneven, and often isolated. It includes a few unusually old foundation outliers as well as younger, stalled, abandoned, and system-scale settlements. |

These ranges overlap; they are descriptive layers rather than borders. The 390-ly reach is a characteristic maximum at the beginning of FTL, with thin arms and gaps rather than an occupied shell. In particular, the outer sublight fringe must not be treated as uniformly mature merely because a few exceptional systems there have long histories.

## Timescale Check

For a direct first-order foundation voyage, the simple transit time is:

$$
t_{\mathrm{arrival}} = D/v_{\mathrm{seed}}.
$$

At an exceptional `0.1c`, destinations at 50, 100, 200, 300, and 390 ly take about 500, 1,000, 2,000, 3,000, and 3,900 years to reach. In an eight-thousand-year history, even a 390-ly outlier can therefore possess roughly four millennia of history before allowing for its founding phase.

A settlement requires an additional, variable **150--500 years** before it can plausibly finance and sustain substantial outward projects. That delay, and the tendency for local networks to saturate or choose consolidation, prevents the rare rapid foundation from making all of its outward directions equally old or densely settled.

## Information, Exchange, and Autonomy

At distance `D` ly, a signal from Sol takes `D` years, an answer takes a further `D` years, and ordinary physical traffic takes roughly `20D` years. A system 100 ly away can receive a Sol-origin research result after a century, but cannot participate in ordinary rapid administration; routine migration or cargo takes about two millennia.

Sol's early influence is therefore chiefly informational and preparatory. It can supply authenticated archives, engineering designs, software, education, cultural material, scientific updates, founding contracts, interoperability standards, and cryptographic institutional credentials. It can attach conditions to financing before departure and confer prestige or restricted technical access later. It cannot sustain real-time command, rapid enforcement, or a uniformly administered pre-FTL political economy across the diaspora.

Digital goods retain value through freshness, provenance, certification, compatibility, and continuing research rather than simple scarcity of copies. Bulk goods do not normally support interstellar trade; physical exchange is limited to exceptionally valuable, compact, strategic, or biologically specific cargoes, and unfolds over generations.

## Divergence at Reconnection

Pre-FTL isolation does not require total silence. It means that contact is too delayed, thin, or unreliable to coordinate ordinary politics, emergencies, markets, or personal lives. The resulting divergence is primarily social and institutional: legitimacy, kinship, property, labour, ecological practice, relations with engineered bodies, attitudes toward automation, and historical memory.

Biological difference is substantial but bounded by shared ancestry and material constraints: adapted populations, inherited engineered traits, local medical traditions, and distinct bodily norms rather than biologically predetermined cultures. Technological divergence likewise consists chiefly of uneven industrial capacity, maintenance traditions, scientific institutions, standards, and specialist strengths, not incompatible physical laws or arbitrary alternate technology trees.

FTL reconnection later creates common standards, migration, and technological diffusion, but it does not erase the accumulated legitimacy or local identity of old societies.

## Consequences for Later Work

- The true core is relevant through density, accumulated institutions, historical prestige, capital, research, and route access—not because it directly governs all space.
- A major legacy civilisation-complex can be internally diverse and politically fragmented while still being a meaningful historical category.
- Population counts, the number of inhabited systems, specific polities, and the distribution of particular lineages remain open.
- [[05 Settlement Topology and Connectivity|Settlement Topology and Connectivity]] owns the selected distinction among mapped, reachable, settled, and integrated space, as well as the pattern of dense clusters, corridors, and meaningful gaps.
- The unresolved FTL communication model still determines how completely reconnection can overcome light-speed information latency.
