## Metric-Gradient Gravity Plating

**Status: Exploring**
**Approval: Required before canonisation**

*Grav plating* is a structural system of opposed **metric-gradient emitters**. Emitters distributed through a deck and its associated frame establish a bounded acceleration profile. That profile defines a local "down" for occupants, cargo, and equipment; it is not a universal direction in space, nor a substance contained in the deck.

Each installation has a rated **field load**: the protected volume, chosen apparent gravity, and total mass-energy being accelerated. A more heavily occupied or loaded volume requires greater power and places greater demands on its emitters and structural return paths. The deck is therefore infrastructure with capacity limits, not a passive convenience: cargo holds, medical bays, emergency shelters, and damaged compartments may need different operating ratings.

The field's reaction forces return through the plating and vessel structure. It gives an occupant weight, while imparting the corresponding force to the installation; the occupants, emitters, field, and hull remain a single momentum-conserving system. A vessel cannot use its own grav plating as reactionless propulsion or as an alternative to a separate inertial-management system.

The remaining design questions are practical rather than a selected FTL mechanism: safe transition zones between fields, alignment and overload protection, the consequences of partial failure, and when spin gravity is cheaper or more resilient than plating. The broader, still-provisional technology family is in [[99 Workshop/02 Spacetime Engineering|Spacetime Engineering]].
