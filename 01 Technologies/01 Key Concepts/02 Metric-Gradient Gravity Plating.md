# Metric-Gradient Gravity Plating

**Type: Setting reference**

**Status: Exploring**

**Approval: Required before canonisation**

**Physical basis: Setting-specific physics**

**Setting role: Active design direction**

*Grav plating* is a structural system of opposed **metric-gradient emitters** ( *NOTE : better name pending* ). Emitters distributed through a deck and its associated frame establish a bounded acceleration profile. That profile defines a local "down" for occupants, cargo, and equipment; it is not a universal direction in space, nor a substance contained in the deck.

Each installation has a rated **field load**: the protected volume, chosen apparent gravity, and total mass-energy being accelerated. A more heavily occupied or loaded volume requires greater power and places greater demands on its emitters and structural return paths. The deck is therefore infrastructure with capacity limits, not a passive convenience: cargo holds, medical bays, emergency shelters, and damaged compartments may need different operating ratings.

The field's reaction forces return through the plating and vessel structure. It gives an occupant weight, while imparting the corresponding force to the installation; the occupants, emitters, field, and hull remain a single momentum-conserving system. A vessel cannot use its own grav plating as reactionless propulsion or as an alternative to a separate inertial-management system ( *NOTE : explore using multidirectional grav plating to counteract jerk motion dynamically* ).

The remaining design questions are practical rather than a selected FTL mechanism: safe transition zones between fields, alignment and overload protection, the consequences of partial failure, and when spin gravity is cheaper or more resilient than plating. The broader, still-provisional technology family is in [[01 Technologies/01 Key Concepts/01 Spacetime Engineering|Spacetime Engineering]].

## Shipboard Gravity Arrangements

### Limited Gravity Plating

As a proposed mature, lower-power application of spacetime engineering, gravity plating is intended to be common enough aboard major vessels that ordinary walking in large non-rotating spaces is familiar. It remains constrained by power use, field coverage, maintenance, and failure modes, so gravity is useful infrastructure rather than invisible background.

This preference favours **limited gravity plating** over pure spin gravity. Rotating sections may still exist where they are more efficient or resilient, but are not required throughout a large vessel.

### Spin Gravity

Habitation modules rotate to provide ordinary centrifugal gravity. This is more familiar physics, but damage, docking, mixed-species accessibility, and a non-rotating bridge become visible design constraints.

### Hybrid Gravity

Rotating habitat sections provide the baseline environment, while limited gravity plating stabilises non-rotating work spaces, medical areas, corridors, docking interfaces, or emergencies. This preserves practical consequences while avoiding the requirement that all ordinary ship life depends on one exotic system.

## Open Design Questions

- What ratings, transition zones, or structural safeguards prevent an overloaded or misaligned metric gradient from injuring occupants or damaging cargo?
- Does the ship use full plating, spin habitats, or a hybrid model?

The common-plating preference and spin/hybrid options remain exploratory. [[03 Ship and Crew/01 Vessel, Crew, and Emergency Organisation|The vessel reference]] owns the eventual installed arrangement and the zones surviving the displacement; this document owns the underlying mechanics.
