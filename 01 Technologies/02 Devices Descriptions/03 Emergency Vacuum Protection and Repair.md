# Emergency Vacuum Protection and Repair

## Current Foundation

Rapid depressurisation is a recurring shipboard hazard. Emergency equipment can improve survival and buy time for rescue, but does not make vacuum harmless or remove the need for sealed compartments, training, and rapid damage control.

### Emergency Vacuum Safety Suit (EVSS)

An EVSS is a compact personal survival system that applies mechanical counter-pressure to the body during a depressurisation emergency. Configurations trade convenience and mobility against coverage and survival margin. Deployable protection for the head and extremities can extend a partly worn suit into more complete emergency coverage.

An EVSS is not a complete substitute for an EVA suit. It needs a compatible breathing system, eye and airway protection, and appropriate thermal management. Thin emergency protection provides little meaningful shielding from ionising radiation.

### Respiratory Interfaces

Some people use an implanted or externally fitted airway interface that can rapidly connect their lungs to an emergency respirator. The draft term *biomechanical inhalator* is retained as a possible colloquial or technical name; its exact implementation and species-specific variants remain open.

### Deployable Vacuum-Protection Shrouds

The draft concept of extendable vacuum-protection bags is retained as deployable shrouds that seal around vulnerable body regions when an EVSS is incomplete. Their exact deployment method and use with different bodies remain open.

### Vacuum Reactive Welding (VRW)

VRW is a working term for a repair process that prepares damaged metal surfaces and uses a controlled reactive or bonding interface to form a durable join in vacuum. The underlying metallurgy remains open. It should not be described as literal generic "metal glue" or rely on unspecified radioactive decay unless that mechanism is later made physically and operationally credible.

### External Repair Enclosures

Flexible, sealable external enclosures can be attached around a damaged hull section to create a temporary controlled work volume. They may contain dust, heat, fragments, tools, and a locally managed atmosphere during repair. They are useful but cumbersome infrastructure, not a universal answer to major structural damage.

## Open Design Questions

- How quickly can an EVSS be worn or deployed, and what degree of decompression can each configuration survive?
- Which crew species require wholly different emergency systems?
- Does a respiratory interface require prior surgery, and how common is voluntary implantation?
- What failures can an external repair enclosure safely address, and what failures require abandoning the compartment or the ship section?
- What physical process makes VRW fast enough for emergency repair without making conventional manufacturing obsolete?

## Intended Use

This document owns the initial emergency-vacuum and repair concepts. A future life-support reference should own the broader physiology, compartmentalisation, atmosphere, and rescue doctrine.
