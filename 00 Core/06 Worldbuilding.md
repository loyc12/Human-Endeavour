# Recommended Build Order

0. Core premise and narrative promise
1. Physics, FTL, and communication constraints
2. The vessel, crew, damage, and survival economy
3. The anomaly and the displacement boundary
4. The immediate Andromedan environment and navigational situation
5. First-contact conditions and local societies
6. Institutions, power, and interstellar political geography
7. History, technological development, and species ecology
8. Current tensions, relationships, and story arcs

---

# 1. Physics, FTL, and Communication Constraints

Define the recurring pressures that make interstellar life and the return problem meaningful.

Focus on:

- FTL travel and navigation
- Relativistic, causal, and communications limits
- Energy, heat, mass, and reaction-mass economics where relevant
- Sensors, stealth, and information limits
- Habitation and environmental hazards
- What the anomaly can and cannot do

> Civilisations and voyages emerge as solutions to physical and logistical problems.

---

# 2. Vessel, Crew, and Survival Economy

Determine what the protagonists can actually sustain and what they must obtain, repair, or sacrifice.

Define:

- Mission and legal mandate
- Crew composition, authority, expertise, and culture
- Damage, redundancy, fabrication, medicine, food, energy, and consumables
- Transport, repair, and manufacturing capabilities
- Information gaps and decision-making under uncertainty
- The social effects of confinement, grief, duty, and unequal risk

> The ship's limits generate its first hard choices.

---

# 3. Immediate Andromedan Situation

Define only the region the ship can plausibly reach before attempting a galaxy-wide catalogue.

Focus on:

- Location, stellar neighbourhood, and useful routes
- Local hazards and resources
- Signals, artefacts, infrastructure, or inhabited systems
- What the crew can know reliably at arrival
- Why remaining, moving on, or revealing themselves each carries risk

> Build outward from the people the story can actually encounter.

---

# 4. First Contact and Local Societies

Design contact conditions before broad political maps.

For each society or contact, define:

- Biology, sensory world, communication, and practical accessibility
- Material conditions, infrastructure, and technology
- Institutions, authority, and internal disagreement
- What they want, fear, misunderstand, and cannot afford to lose
- Their likely interpretation of the vessel and its crew
- Mutual risks, obligations, and possible grounds for trust

> First contact is a relationship between particular people under unequal uncertainty, not a meeting of monoliths.

---

# 5. Interstellar Institutions and Power

Expand from local contacts to the structures that constrain them.

Define:

- Sources of legitimacy and authority
- Law, trade, migration, borders, and sovereignty
- Scientific, military, commercial, religious, and civic institutions where relevant
- Transportation and communications networks
- Political factions, dependencies, and historical grievances
- How large-scale power is felt in the immediate region

> Politics emerge from competing institutions.

---

# 6. Species, Cultures, and History

Develop these in response to specific narrative needs rather than as an encyclopaedia of a galaxy.

- Design species from evolutionary and environmental realities, then trace their practical effects without turning biology into destiny.
- Let cultures arise from material conditions, shared histories, institutions, and contact—not from species archetypes.
- Build history as accumulated technological, ecological, economic, and political change.
- Give every broad fact a local expression: a custom, bottleneck, memory, law, infrastructure system, or tension the protagonists can encounter.

> History explains the present rather than existing for its own sake.

---

# 7. Current Tensions and Story Arcs

Every major element should generate conflict.

For each organization, society or polity define:

- Goals and fears
- Resources and vulnerabilities
- Dependencies and obligations
- Rivals, allies, and internal disagreements
- External pressures and information gaps
- What changes if the protagonists act, refuse to act, or leave

> Stable worlds are built from unstable relationships.

---

# General Principles

- Build causes before outcomes.
- Let civilisations emerge from constraints.
- Let institutions shape politics.
- Let species have practical biological differences without determining culture.
- Let economics drive incentives.
- Let history accumulate naturally.
- Prefer conflicts of values, knowledge, incentives, and need over simplistic moral sorting.
- Design interactions before isolated factions.
- Every major system should influence several others.
- Every worldbuilding decision should create downstream consequences.

## Intended Use

This is a dependency guide, not a requirement to complete every tier before writing scenes. Prioritise the next unresolved constraint that will change several later decisions or directly support the current narrative work.
