# Core Premise

| Aspect                  | Current foundation                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Genre                   | Character-driven, hard science-fiction exploration and survival story.                                                                                                                                |
| Core situation          | An [[00 Core/07 Terminology|interspecies]] exploratory vessel is caught in a space-time anomaly and deposited in the Andromeda galaxy, far beyond practical support from its home region.              |
| Immediate problem       | The crew must keep a damaged, finite, highly capable vessel and its people alive while determining where they are, building local relationships, and deciding what a responsible route home could be. |
| Central dramatic engine | Survival pressures and first contacts force a diverse crew to cooperate across species, cultures, expertise, trauma, and conflicting duties.                                                          |
| Tone                    | Hopeful but grounded: catastrophe has lasting costs; care, ingenuity, and cooperation can still produce meaningful gains.                                                                             |
| Long-term direction     | The possibility of returning home exists as a driving question, not an assumed solution. The journey itself must become a life with obligations and consequences.                                     |
| Original mission        | A governmental pilot programme tests whether a large, long-duration vessel can sustain genuinely integrated multispecies life. Exploration is a secondary objective.                                  |

## Established Constraints

- The vessel was not sent to Andromeda intentionally.
- The programme is sponsored by a [[00 Core/07 Terminology|human-led coalition]] with significant participation from non-human polities. The coalition's final name and constitutional structure remain open.
- The programme is controversial, but small enough in cost and political scope that its supporters can sustain it. Its design is imperfect rather than intentionally supremacist: human requirements are the most tested, most common among the crew, and best represented among its builders, so conflicting requirements often resolve to a human default with accommodations.
- The crew includes baseline humans, heavily modified human descendants, biological-cybernetic people, rare synthetic intelligences, and uplifted species. An interspecies vessel is one that can safely sustain people whose bodily and environmental needs cannot be met by a human-default habitat; see [[00 Core/07 Terminology|Terminology]].
- The mission combines civilian and enlisted personnel. Long, normally low-risk voyages require substantial civilian-like work as well as safety and defensive capability; vetted family and clan relations are therefore permitted aboard.
- Most known sapient species share an entangled history with human expansion or uplift. At least one known species has an independent, though limited, technological history.
- The displacement severely damages the ship, causes heavy loss of life, and removes the original captain from effective command. The captain's ultimate fate remains open.
- The ship passes through a spatial tear in which violent motion and debris cause much of the damage. The tear's mechanism, debris source, and connection to the destination wreckage field remain open.
- The anomaly is a physical phenomenon whose effects must obey the setting axioms, even if the crew cannot initially explain or reproduce it.
- The crew cannot rely on timely help, resupply, or ordinary communication from home. The precise communications and causality boundary is still to be defined.
- The vessel's advanced systems confer options, not immunity from fuel, damage, maintenance, information gaps, social strain, or ethical responsibility.
- Andromeda contains civilisations with more disparate origins and less shared history than the Milky Way. Its societies are not presumed to be technologically, biologically, morally, or politically uniform.
- A direct return through ordinary FTL would be a multigenerational, multi-century undertaking. Finding an alternative is therefore preferable, but must not be easy or consequence-free.

## Worldbuilding Principle

The setting aims for **realism in its consequences**. Given premises ( especially the constrained FTL, the ship's finite capabilities, the anomalous displacement, and independent civilisations ) their social, material, political, and historical effects should flow coherently from one another rather than accumulate as unrelated conventions.

Coherence is a constraint, not an instruction to exhaust every plausible implication. When several consequences are equally credible, prefer the one that is simpler to express, creates useful connections with established material, and is more interesting for the story.

## Open Foundations

The following questions are intentionally unresolved and should be answered before detailed technical, political, or historical canon depends on them:

- What is the coalition called, what authority does it have, and what legal mandate and safeguards govern the pilot programme?
- What was the programme's actual duration and risk profile, and how do civilian, enlisted, family, and clan relationships work aboard it?
- What did the anomaly do besides displacement, and why does its exit contain a wreckage field?
- How do FTL travel, interstellar communication, navigation, and energy supply work, and which limitations make the return journey difficult rather than impossible?
- Which Andromedan civilisations or historical paths make the first region compelling and distinct from the Milky Way?
- What does a credible route home require, and what moral or strategic costs could pursuing it impose?
