# Inspirations

## Intended Use

Use these influences when choosing among compatible possibilities, especially where they clarify the setting's hopeful-but-grounded tone or its hard-SF consequences. Record the resulting decision in the relevant workshop or reference document rather than treating an inspiration as evidence that a decision has already been made.

These are author-facing influences and reference points. They provide useful questions, tonal direction, and structural patterns; they do not import their canon, technologies, institutions, chronology, or moral conclusions into *Human Endeavour*.

## Isaac Arthur

Isaac Arthur's futurist work is a reference for taking space settlement, infrastructure, energy, habitation, industrial capacity, logistics, automation, biology, and long time-scales seriously. It supports asking how a technology works at civilisational scale and how ordinary people live with its material constraints, not assuming any one proposed technology or future is correct.

## Star Trek

Star Trek's hopeful futurism is a tonal and ethical reference: diverse people can build genuine cooperation, institutions can improve, and exploration can be curious, principled, and materially useful. Retain the possibility of competence, solidarity, and reform without assuming effortless abundance, political unity, or the absence of durable injustice.

## Star Wars

Star Wars' pre-prequel political imagination is a reference for a civilisation connected by institutions, commerce, travel, and shared political language while remaining too large, unequal, and regionally varied to function as a simple unitary state. Use it to ask about legitimacy, representation, peripheral power, institutional drift, and the gap between formal equality and practical influence; do not reproduce a particular polity, constitution, or historical arc.
